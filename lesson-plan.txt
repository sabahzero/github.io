            <!-- ===== PREPARATION / PLANNING ===== -->
            <h2>Preparation / Planning</h2>
            <div class="meta-block">
                <p><strong>ESL Course:</strong> English for Everyday Life</p>
                <p><strong>Level:</strong> Intermediate </p>
                <p><strong>Topic / Theme:</strong> Sustainable Living Practices</p>
            </div>

            <h3>Objectives</h3>
            <ul>
                <li><strong>Listening:</strong> Students listen to a short teacher presentation and peer discussions to identify key information about sustainable practices.</li>
                <li><strong>Speaking:</strong> Students discuss sustainable practices in pairs and small groups using sentence frames (e.g., “___ is a sustainable practice because ___,” “One way to reduce waste is ___”).</li>
                <li><strong>Reading:</strong> Students read a 150–200 word text about sustainable practices and identify main ideas and supporting details.</li>
                <li><strong>Writing:</strong> Students complete a graphic organizer identifying sustainable practices and their benefits, and write an exit ticket response.</li>
            </ul>

            <div class="divider"></div>

            <!-- ===== BRIDGING ===== -->
            <h2>Bridging</h2>
            <h3>What background knowledge do the students already have?</h3>
            <p>Students come from diverse cultural and linguistic backgrounds. Many have prior knowledge of environmental issues, such as recycling, pollution, or conservation, but may not have the academic vocabulary in English to discuss these concepts. Some students may have experience with sustainability practices in their home countries (e.g., traditional practices of reusing materials, conserving water, or reducing waste) and can draw on these experiences. Others may be less familiar with the specific language and concepts.</p>

            <h3>What will you do to activate or link students' prior knowledge or experience to upcoming content?</h3>
            <p>I will begin with a Think-Pair-Share activity asking students to discuss: “What does it mean to live sustainably?” This activates prior knowledge and allows students to share their own experiences. I will use a K-W-L chart (Know-Want to Know-Learned) to elicit what students already know about sustainability and what they want to learn. I will also use visuals (photographs of recycling, solar panels, community gardens) to prompt discussion and connect to students' lived experiences. To build on existing knowledge, I will ask: “In your country/culture, what do people do to take care of the environment?” This validates students' cultural knowledge and creates a bridge to new content.</p>

            <div class="divider"></div>

            <!-- ===== ENGAGEMENT WITH NEW MATERIAL ===== -->
            <h2>Engagement with New Material</h2>
            <h3>What will you do to engage students in the active learning of the new material?</h3>
            <p>I will use a variety of strategies to engage students actively:</p>
            <ul>
                <li><strong>Vocabulary sorting activity:</strong> Students will match vocabulary cards (sustainable, recycle, conserve, carbon footprint, etc.) with definitions and images in pairs, promoting kinesthetic and visual learning.</li>
                <li><strong>Guided reading with think-aloud:</strong> I will model reading the text aloud, pausing to think aloud about main ideas and highlighting key vocabulary. Students will then read in pairs and answer comprehension questions.</li>
                <li><strong>Jigsaw reading (extension):</strong> If time permits, I will use a jigsaw approach where each group reads a different section (e.g., reducing waste, conserving water, renewable energy) and teaches others.</li>
                <li><strong>Graphic organizer:</strong> Students will complete a visual organizer identifying sustainable practices and their environmental benefits, helping structure their understanding.</li>
            </ul>

            <h3>What will you do to ensure that all students are engaged?</h3>
            <p>To ensure all students are engaged, I will:</p>
            <ul>
                <li><strong>Differentiate materials:</strong> Provide texts at varying reading levels (simplified and more complex versions) and use sentence frames for speaking and writing.</li>
                <li><strong>Use multiple grouping strategies:</strong> Include whole-class, partner (think-pair-share), and small-group work to reach different learners.</li>
                <li><strong>Incorporate various modalities:</strong> Use visual (pictures, graphic organizers), auditory (teacher modeling, discussions), and kinesthetic (sorting cards, matching activities) approaches to appeal to different learning styles.</li>
                <li><strong>Provide scaffolding:</strong> Offer vocabulary lists with pictures, sentence frames for speaking and writing, and partially completed organizers for students who need more support.</li>
                <li><strong>Monitor participation:</strong> Circulate during pair and group work to ensure all students are participating and offer support as needed.</li>
            </ul>

            <div class="divider"></div>

            <!-- ===== APPLICATION ===== -->
            <h2>Application</h2>
            <h3>What opportunities will you provide students to practice and apply their knowledge/skill to meet the objectives for this lesson? To apply to other contexts?</h3>
            <p>Students will have multiple opportunities to practice and apply what they've learned:</p>
            <ul>
                <li><strong>Practice opportunity 1:</strong> In pairs, students complete a graphic organizer identifying three sustainable practices and their benefits. They use the text and vocabulary cards for reference.</li>
                <li><strong>Practice opportunity 2:</strong> In small groups (3–4 students), students discuss the question: “What sustainable practice do you think is most important, and why?” They use sentence frames such as “I think ______ is most important because ______” and “In my country, we ______.” This encourages the use of target language and builds speaking confidence.</li>
                <li><strong>Application to other contexts:</strong> To apply their learning beyond the classroom, I will challenge students to consider sustainability in their daily lives by asking:
                    <ul>
                        <li>“What is one sustainable practice you could start doing in your daily life?”</li>
                        <li>“What sustainable practices do you see in your community?”</li>
                        <li>“How do sustainable practices differ across cultures?”</li>
                        <li>As an extension, students could research a sustainability issue in their home country and present their findings in the next class.</li>
                    </ul>
                </li>
            </ul>

            <h4>Authentic, Project-Based Extension (to enhance real-world application and encourage continued language use beyond the classroom):</h4>
            <ul>
                <li><strong>Option 1: Personal Sustainability Action Plan.</strong> Students will create a one-week personal sustainability action plan. They will identify one sustainable practice they will commit to (e.g., reducing plastic use, conserving water, composting), write specific steps they will take each day, and document their progress. At the end of the week, students will share their experiences in a short presentation or journal reflection, using language such as “I planned to ______,” “I was able to ______,” and “One challenge I faced was ______.”</li>
                <li><strong>Option 2: Community Awareness Poster.</strong> Students will work in small groups to design a community awareness poster promoting a sustainable practice relevant to their local community (e.g., recycling, energy conservation, community gardening). The poster must include a clear message, at least three supporting reasons, and visual elements. Groups will present their posters to the class, explaining their message and why it matters. Posters can be displayed around the school or community center.</li>
                <li><strong>Option 3: Sustainability Interview Project.</strong> Students will interview a family member, neighbor, or community member about sustainable practices in their culture or community. They will prepare 3–5 questions (e.g., “What sustainable practices did you grow up with?” “How have these practices changed over time?”), conduct the interview in English, and present their findings to the class. This project integrates listening, speaking, reading, and writing while connecting language learning to authentic community engagement.</li>
            </ul>

            <p>I will structure the closing in three parts:</p>

I will explicitly connect back to the content and language objectives, saying: “Today we learned about sustainable practices and practiced explaining why they are important.”</li>
                <li><strong>Exit ticket activity:</strong> Students will complete the exit ticket independently, providing me with assessment data and allowing them to consolidate their learning.</li>
                <li><strong>Preview and connect to larger picture:</strong> I will preview the next lesson, saying: “Next time, we'll explore how sustainable practices differ across cultures and discuss ways to promote sustainability in our communities.” I will also introduce the upcoming project-based extension (e.g., Personal Sustainability Action Plan or Community Awareness Poster), explaining the purpose, timeline, and expectations. This helps students see the lesson as part of a larger unit and builds anticipation for future learning.</li>

            <h2>Rationale &amp; Overview</h2>
            <p>My lesson plan is designed for an intermediate-level class of adult learners (ages 18–30) of English, focusing on sustainable living practices. The lesson follows a backward design approach, beginning with clear learning objectives and working backward to create assessments and activities that support student understanding. For the opening, I planned a Think-Pair-Share activity where students discuss, “What does it mean to live sustainably?” This activates prior knowledge, builds background, and creates a low-stakes opportunity for students to practice speaking before I clearly state the content and language objectives on the board. This opening should take approximately 7 minutes. The activities are sequenced to gradually build understanding from receptive to productive skills: Vocabulary introduction (10 minutes), guided reading (10 minutes), pair work graphic organizer (12 minutes), group discussion (10 minutes), and closing (6 minutes). Each activity builds on the previous one, and all four skills are integrated throughout.</p>
            <p>Regarding pacing, I have allocated approximately 55 minutes total with built-in transition time between activities. This ensures students have sufficient time to process new information and practice language meaningfully while allowing flexibility for student needs. For the closing, I planned a structured exit ticket where students answer, “What is one sustainable practice you learned about today?” and “Why is this practice important?” using differentiated sentence starters. I will then summarize main points, explicitly connect back to the content and language objectives, and preview the next lesson. This closing should take approximately 6 minutes and will provide formative assessment data to guide future instruction. To extend learning beyond the classroom, students will complete a project-based extension such as a Personal Sustainability Action Plan or Community Awareness Poster, allowing them to apply their knowledge in authentic, real-world contexts.</p>

        (function() {
            const toggle = document.getElementById('themeToggle');
            const icon = document.getElementById('themeIcon');
            const root = document.documentElement;

            const stored = localStorage.getItem('theme');
            const prefersDark = window.matchMedia('(prefers-color-scheme: dark)').matches;

                const next = currentTheme === 'light' ? 'dark' : 'light';
                setTheme(next);
            });

            window.matchMedia('(prefers-color-scheme: dark)').addEventListener('change', (e) => {
                if (!localStorage.getItem('theme')) {
                    setTheme(e.matches ? 'dark' : 'light');
                }
            });
        })();
